# Potential Funders for the Structural Alloy SDL Workshop

This document catalogs potential funding sources for a workshop (and follow-on
research activities) at the intersection of **self-driving laboratories (SDLs)**
and **structural alloys** (metallic materials for load-bearing,
high-temperature, fatigue-critical, and extreme-environment applications).

The list is organized by funder, with the most directly relevant programs first.
For each program we note the **scope**, **why it fits an SDL-for-alloys
workshop**, and pointers for **identifying the current Program Officer / Program
Manager (PO/PM)**. Specific PO/PM names are intentionally *not* hard-coded here
because rotators change frequently (typically every 1–4 years at NSF, and on
similar cycles at DOD/DOE); always confirm the current cognizant officer on the
agency's program page before reaching out.

> Convention used below: **NSF** uses "Program Officer" (PO) or "Program
> Director" (PD); **DOD/DOE/DARPA/ARPA-E** use "Program Manager" (PM). The
> issue title's "PM" is used loosely to mean "the cognizant program contact."

## 1. National Science Foundation (NSF) — Highest Priority

NSF is the most natural primary sponsor for an academic workshop on SDLs for
structural alloys. Workshops are typically funded as small (~$50–100K)
**conference/workshop proposals** under the relevant program, often with an
EAGER or RAISE follow-on for the research agenda that emerges.

### 1.1 Directorate for Mathematical & Physical Sciences (MPS) — Division of Materials Research (DMR)

Most relevant DMR programs:

| Program | Relevance to Structural-Alloy SDL |
|---|---|
| **Metals and Metallic Nanostructures (MMN)** | Core program for the science of metallic alloys — processing, structure, mechanical behavior. Direct topical fit. |
| **Designing Materials to Revolutionize and Engineer our Future (DMREF)** | NSF's flagship Materials Genome Initiative (MGI) program. Explicitly funds iterative, data-driven, autonomous discovery loops — the exact paradigm of an SDL. |
| **Condensed Matter and Materials Theory (CMMT)** | Theory/ML/simulation components of an SDL stack. |
| **Materials Research Science and Engineering Centers (MRSEC)** | Center-scale follow-on if the workshop seeds a multi-PI proposal. |
| **Mid-scale Research Infrastructure (Mid-scale RI-1 / RI-2)** | If the SDL itself becomes a shared instrument (~$0.6M–$100M). |

**Find the current PO:** https://www.nsf.gov/div/index.jsp?div=DMR → "Staff" tab.

### 1.2 Directorate for Engineering (ENG) — Division of Civil, Mechanical and Manufacturing Innovation (CMMI)

| Program | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **Mechanics of Materials and Structures (MoMS)** | Mechanical response, fatigue, fracture of alloys. |
| **Advanced Manufacturing (AM)** | Process–structure–property closed loops for alloy manufacturing (AM/DED/L-PBF, casting, forging). |
| **Engineering Design and Systems Engineering (EDSE)** | The systems/optimization layer of an SDL. |
| **Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)** (cross-Directorate with CISE) | Robotic experimentation + control + ML — the SDL as a CPS. |

**Find the current PO:** https://www.nsf.gov/div/index.jsp?div=CMMI → "Staff" tab.

### 1.3 Directorate for Computer & Information Science & Engineering (CISE)

| Program | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **Cyberinfrastructure for Sustained Scientific Innovation (CSSI)** | Software/data infrastructure for SDLs (orchestration, data schemas, FAIR data). |
| **National AI Research Institutes** | Large-scale AI+materials institutes have been funded; future tracks are plausible. |
| **Foundations of Robotics (FRR)** / **National Robotics Initiative (NRI) follow-ons** | Robotic sample handling and autonomous experimentation. |

### 1.4 Cross-cutting NSF mechanisms worth naming explicitly

- **Conference Proposals** (PAPPG Chapter II.F.9) — the primary vehicle to fund the workshop itself.
- **EAGER** — early-stage, high-risk follow-on (≤$300K, ≤2 yrs).
- **RAISE** — convergent, cross-Directorate ideas (≤$1M, ≤5 yrs); good fit for SDL+alloys spanning DMR/CMMI/CISE.
- **GOALI** — if industrial alloy producers (e.g., aerospace/auto OEMs) co-PI.
- **Convergence Accelerator** — track-based, use-inspired; recent tracks have included AI/ML for science.
- **Global Centers / IMPRESS-U** — international collaboration on materials/AI.

## 2. Department of Energy (DOE)

### 2.1 Office of Science — Basic Energy Sciences (BES)

- **Materials Sciences and Engineering Division (MSED)**
  - *Mechanical Behavior and Radiation Effects* — directly relevant to structural alloys for energy systems.
  - *Synthesis and Processing Science* — autonomous synthesis/processing.
- **Computational Materials Sciences (CMS)** — autonomous-experimentation software stacks.
- **DOE National Lab user facilities** as in-kind partners (APS, NSLS-II, SNS, HFIR, CNMS, Molecular Foundry, NCEM) — many already host or are building SDL-style beamline/microscopy autonomy.

### 2.2 ARPA-E

Look for solicitations in **structural materials for extreme environments**
(historically e.g., ULTIMATE for turbine alloys, ROSIE/EVOLVE-style programs).
ARPA-E PMs actively scope new programs from community workshops — co-locating
or summarizing the workshop output for ARPA-E is high leverage.

### 2.3 Advanced Materials & Manufacturing Technologies Office (AMMTO, formerly AMO)

Applied-side funding for alloy manufacturing scale-up and digital/AI-enabled
manufacturing.

## 3. Department of Defense (DOD)

Structural alloys are a long-standing core DOD interest (airframes, engines,
hulls, armor, hypersonics). All three service basic-research offices have
relevant portfolios.

| Agency | Relevant portfolios |
|---|---|
| **AFOSR** (Air Force Office of Scientific Research) | *Aerospace Materials for Extreme Environments*; *Dynamic Materials & Interactions*; *Computational Mathematics*. Strong recent interest in autonomous experimentation. |
| **ONR** (Office of Naval Research) | Code 332 *Naval Materials S&T* (structural metals for marine/aero); Code 31 *Mathematics, Computers, Information Sciences*. |
| **ARO** (Army Research Office) | Materials Science Division — *Synthesis & Processing*, *Mechanical Behavior of Materials*; Computing Sciences Division for AI/autonomy. |
| **DARPA** | Historically: *Open Manufacturing*, *Materials Development for Platforms (MDP)*, *Make-It*, *Accelerated Molecular Discovery*. New PMs cycle in ~3-yr terms; monitor https://www.darpa.mil/about/offices for DSO and MTO. |
| **DEVCOM ARL / AFRL / NRL** | In-house labs that frequently co-fund and co-host workshops. |

DOD basic-research offices fund workshops via **conference grants** and
single-PI BAAs; PM relationships are typically built before a proposal.

## 4. NIST

- **Material Measurement Laboratory (MML)** — Materials Genome Initiative
  coordination; CHiMaD-style centers of excellence.
- **NIST Center for Automated Materials Discovery / autonomous research
  programs** — natural collaborator and potential co-host.
- **NIST grants programs** (e.g., to academic partners) and **CHIPS R&D**
  metrology funding for advanced packaging materials are tangentially relevant.

---

## 5. NASA

- **Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD)** — Early Stage Innovation,
  ESI / Early Career Faculty awards on structural materials for space
  (high-temperature alloys, refractory alloys, additive manufacturing).
- **Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate (ARMD)** — Transformational Tools
  and Technologies project for materials & ICME.

## 6. Other federal & quasi-federal

- **DOE NNSA** (Kansas City NSC, LANL, LLNL, SNL) — structural materials for
  weapons/energy stockpile; autonomous characterization initiatives.
- **NRC** (Nuclear Regulatory Commission) — fellowships/faculty development
  relevant to nuclear structural alloys.
- **Manufacturing USA institutes** — esp. **America Makes** (additive
  manufacturing of alloys) and **LIFT** (lightweight metals).

## 9. Action items for the workshop organizers

1. **Shortlist 3–5 NSF POs** across DMR (MMN, DMREF), CMMI (MoMS, AM), and
   CISE (CSSI) using the staff pages linked above; send a 1-page workshop
   concept *before* drafting the conference proposal.
2. **Identify 1–2 DOD PMs** (AFOSR aerospace materials + ONR Code 332) for
   potential co-funding; DOD often appreciates being approached early.
3. **Reach out to ARPA-E** and **TRI** for industry-aligned co-sponsorship.
4. **Confirm current PO/PM names and emails** on each agency's program page at
   the time of outreach — do not rely on names from older proposals or this
   document.
5. Track outreach in a separate (non-public) contacts spreadsheet; this file
   should remain a public catalog of *programs*, not individuals.
